The queued parser fix closes only the first problem. The other two keep coming in after it ships.

How the reports split

  • parser. The shared parser reads every date month first, and every tenant in #4471 writes the day first. The day and month swap: a 7 April reminder goes out on 4 July. Any channel, any tenant.
  • hint. The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field but reads the day first. People type what the hint tells them, and the date swaps. Only the form channel of booking-v3 tenants. Fixing the parser does not fix this; the hint has to change.
  • shift. Not a swap at all: the date moves by one day, and where the reader sits decides which day they get (“Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday. Same meeting.”). The desk noted these as a one-day move, but the cause table counts them under parser by tenant. It looks like a date-only value passed through a time zone conversion.

How to tell them apart at intake

  1. Look up the tenant, not the reporter’s browser. The intake tag is a guess and disagrees with the tenant on dozens of reports in #4471.
  2. Form channel on a booking-v3 tenant: hint.
  3. The two dates in the body are one day apart, not swapped: shift.
  4. Anything else from a day-first tenant: parser.

Matching on subject is still fine for speed. Before merging into a big ticket, check that the cause matches too.

Lessons

  • A subject line is a symptom. Three bugs can share one.
  • A note in the margin (“read as a one-day move”) is a ticket nobody opened.
